Brand guidelines Version 2.0, October 2026

The identity for Quillmarc: investor updates, written from Stripe. Calm, exact and finished, like the letter it writes.

Principles

Four words decide every choice.

Quillmarc sits between a founder and the people who backed them. The brand should feel like a well-kept account book and a carefully written letter: nothing loud, nothing vague, everything finished.

01

Calm

Generous space and one clear action per screen. Nothing blinks, shouts or competes for attention.

02

Precise

Real numbers that add up, exact words, tabular figures. Never a vague claim or a rounded boast.

03

Premium

Restraint and finish: good type, true colour, careful detail. Quality shown, not announced.

04

Editorial

Set like a publication. Serif headlines, measured paragraphs, the product shown like a printed page.

Logo

A pen-written Q, and gold where the pen stops.

The wordmark opens with the pen-written Q, its tail ending in a small gold square, and the same square dots the “i”. The square is the brand’s signature: a full stop, the update finished. Where the name will not fit, the Q stands alone.

  1. The bowl. A circle minus a tilted ellipse, so the stroke swells and thins the way a broad nib does. Never replace it with an even ring.
  2. The tail. Starts inside the bowl, crosses the stroke at 45° and tapers as the pen lifts.
  3. The full stop. A square with softened corners, set where the pen stops. Gold in colour versions, ink in one-colour versions. In the wordmark, the same square also dots the “i”.
  4. The grid. Built on 40 units. The bowl is centred at 18, 18 with a radius of 13.

Clear space

Leave at least the height of the “Q” bowl on every side of the wordmark. More is better.

Minimum size

Wordmark 72px wide on screen, 18mm in print. Symbol 16px. Below that, use the symbol alone.

Symbol and wordmark

The Q already leads the wordmark, so never set the standalone symbol beside it. Use the wordmark, or the Q alone.

Colour

Midnight navy, ivory, and a touch of gold.

Ivory and midnight navy carry every page. Sapphire marks the action. Champagne softens surfaces and highlights figures. Gilt gold is the jewellery: the dot on the i, the full stop, a bullet, and the buttons and accents that sit on navy. Roughly 78% ivory, 15% navy, 4% sapphire, 2% champagne and 1% gold.

Voice

Write like a founder who respects the reader’s time.

Plain, specific, unhurried. Every sentence should survive being read by a skeptical investor.

  • Say the specific thing. “Pulls MRR, churn and customers from Stripe” beats “powerful insights”.
  • Real numbers or none. No invented customers, quotes, logos or statistics. Sample data is labelled as fictional.
  • No em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma. Short sentences.
  • Sentence case everywhere. Headings, buttons, labels.
  • Buttons name the action. “Join the waitlist”, the same words every time.
  • Calm, not cute. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no quill puns.

Imagery

The product is the picture.

Show the real interface, crafted at full fidelity, with numbers that add up and a note that the data is a sample. Set it on a midnight navy stage with a faint dot grid. No stock photography, no illustrated rockets or graphs, no device frames.
